\title{\textbf{What Open-Loop Hides and Closed-Loop Reveals:\\
Weight Quantization, Generation Coherence, and On-Device Deployment\\
of Autonomous-Driving Vision--Language--Action Models}}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Justin Williams, Kishor Datta Gupta, Roy George, Mrinmoy Sarkar}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Clark Atlanta University \\ \texttt{justinwilliamstech693@gmail.com}}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (VLA) models are an emerging approach to end-to-end autonomous driving,
but their large language backbones must be compressed before they can run on in-vehicle hardware.
Post-training quantization is the standard tool, yet its consequences for \emph{driving} are
unexamined---and the stakes differ fundamentally from the robot arms where VLA quantization has
been studied: a driving policy shares the road with people, and a single corrupted plan is a
collision. We present the first study of quantized driving VLAs, evaluated three ways: open-loop
on nuScenes, closed-loop in the NeuroNCAP~\cite{neuroncap} photorealistic safety simulator, and on
a real NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin. The three views disagree in an instructive way. Open-loop success
certifies 4-bit quantization as lossless---but we show this metric is dominated by ego-motion
extrapolation and structurally cannot see quantization damage. Under closed-loop safety testing,
the same quantized policy collapses: it stops producing coherent trajectories under the
deployment-domain shift. The failure is governed by quantization scale \emph{granularity}, not
bit-width, and group-wise scales recover FP16-level behavior at negligible memory cost. On the
Orin, the planner deploys comfortably, and both the accuracy and the open-loop metric's blindness
reproduce on the target hardware. Our conclusion for practitioners: quantized driving policies
must be certified closed-loop and on-device---open-loop success is false assurance.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
vision--language--action models, autonomous driving, post-training quantization, group-wise INT4, closed-loop evaluation, generation coherence, edge deployment, Jetson Orin

\section{Introduction}
On-board inference for end-to-end driving stacks motivates compressing the language backbones of
driving VLAs. The prevailing evidence---near-lossless 4-bit weight quantization on open-loop nuScenes
planning---suggests this is essentially free. We show this evidence is structurally unable to support
a safety claim, that closed-loop evaluation tells a different and more actionable story, and that both
conclusions reproduce on real edge hardware. We organize around five questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{Q1}] Can open-loop nuScenes L2 see quantization damage?
\item[\textbf{Q2}] Does quantization affect \emph{closed-loop} behavior, where the logged ego
trajectory cannot be coasted on?
\item[\textbf{Q3}] What property of the quantizer governs the effect, and what is the fix?
\item[\textbf{Q4}] What is the memory/latency profile on an edge accelerator?
\item[\textbf{Q5}] Do the accuracy conclusions hold when the model actually runs on that accelerator?
\end{itemize}
Contributions: a controlled demonstration of open-loop blindness ($19\times$ ego-ablation); the first
closed-loop characterization of weight-quantization effects in a driving VLA; the identification of
scale \emph{granularity} (not bit-width) as the governing factor with a near-free fix; and end-to-end
\emph{on-Orin} systems and accuracy measurements, including a per-scene success breakdown.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\textbf{Driving planners and VLAs.} UniAD~\cite{uniad} introduced a planning-oriented, query-based
perception stack (multi-camera ResNet-101, temporal BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer}, track/map/motion/
occupancy heads). VAD~\cite{vad} vectorized the scene for efficiency. OpenDriveVLA~\cite{opendrivevla}
reuses UniAD track+map perception as a vision tower for a Qwen2.5 language/action head that emits
trajectories as text, reaching state-of-the-art open-loop L2 (Table~\ref{tab:planners}).
\textbf{Open-loop critique.} ``Is Ego Status All You Need?''~\cite{egostatus} showed nuScenes
open-loop planning is dominated by ego state and proposed road-adherence metrics; we quantify the
effect ($19\times$) specifically as it bears on \emph{quantization} evaluation.
\textbf{Closed-loop neural simulation.} NeuroNCAP~\cite{neuroncap} couples a NeuRAD~\cite{neurad}
neural renderer to a safety-test protocol (frontal/side/stationary collision scenarios), enabling
photorealistic closed-loop evaluation and revealing that open-loop-strong planners fail
safety-critical closed-loop tests. \textbf{LLM weight quantization.} Round-to-nearest (RTN),
GPTQ~\cite{gptq} (Hessian-weighted error minimization), and AWQ~\cite{awq} (activation-aware scaling)
differ in how they choose quantization grids; all rely on \emph{group-wise} (block) scales to bound
weight-outlier damage, a practice motivated by the outlier phenomenon in LLMs~\cite{llmint8,
smoothquant}. \textbf{Our positioning.} Prior quantization work targets language benchmarks
(perplexity, MMLU); prior driving-eval work does not study quantization. We are, to our knowledge,
the first to evaluate driving-VLA quantization \emph{closed-loop} and \emph{on-device}, and to show
that the standard open-loop metric certifies a policy the closed-loop test rejects.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Model and quantization scope}
OpenDriveVLA-0.5B pairs Qwen2.5-0.5B (hidden $896$, $24$ layers) with UniAD stage-1 track+map
perception. We apply weight-only post-training quantization to the \textbf{168 Linear layers of the
Qwen backbone ($357.8$\,M parameters)}, leaving perception, the scene/track/map projectors, token
embeddings, and the output head in floating point, so any observed effect is attributable to the
language head's $W_b$ precision. Weights are dequantized to the compute dtype for inference
(\emph{fake-quant}): the arithmetic path is unchanged, so accuracy effects are real while packed
memory/latency gains are projected until a packed kernel is added (Sec.~\ref{sec:orin}).

\subsection{Quantization methodology and why granularity matters}
\label{sec:quantmethod}
\textbf{Round-to-nearest (RTN).} For a weight block $\mathbf{w}$ and $b$ bits, symmetric RTN uses a
single scale $s=\max_i|w_i|/(2^{b-1}-1)$ and maps $\hat w_i = s\cdot\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\mathrm{round}
(w_i/s),\,-2^{b-1},\,2^{b-1}-1\big)$; asymmetric RTN adds a zero-point over $[\min,\max]$. We use RTN
deliberately: it is data-free and introduces \emph{no} optimization of its own, so varying only the
block over which $s$ is shared isolates the effect of \emph{scale granularity}.

\textbf{Granularity.} \emph{Per-output-channel} shares one scale across an entire weight row (all
$896$/intermediate inputs); \emph{group-wise} ($g{=}128$) uses an independent scale per contiguous
block of $g$ input weights. The extra storage is one FP16 scale per group, i.e.\ $16/g$ bits/weight
($0.125$ at $g{=}128$; Table~\ref{tab:mem}).

\textbf{Why group-wise works (the outlier--scale mechanism).} The shared scale is set by the block's
largest magnitude, $s=\max|w|/(2^{b-1}-1)$. A single outlier weight inflates $s$ and therefore
coarsens the quantization grid for \emph{every} small, signal-carrying weight that shares the scale.
The effective per-weight rounding error is $\propto s$; at INT4 the grid has only $15$ levels, so one
outlier in a $896$-wide row can multiply the error of the whole row. Group-wise scales confine each
outlier to its own $128$-weight block, bounding the damage. This is the same rationale behind
LLM outlier handling~\cite{llmint8,smoothquant} and behind the group-wise default in GPTQ and AWQ; our
contribution is to show it is \emph{decisive for closed-loop driving behavior}, not merely for
perplexity. Relation to prior methods (Table~\ref{tab:quantmethods}): GPTQ additionally minimizes a
Hessian-weighted reconstruction error via sequential updates; AWQ additionally rescales salient
channels using activation statistics. Both are complementary to---and layer on top of---the
granularity we isolate; we use plain group-wise RTN to attribute the closed-loop recovery purely to
granularity, and note GPTQ/AWQ would be expected to match or exceed it at equal bits.

\subsection{Open-loop evaluation}
We evaluate on the $162$ nuScenes-mini $\cap$ validation samples under the ST-P3 and UniAD L2
protocols. The \emph{ego-ablation} zeroes the ego-state vector and the $2$\,s history trajectory in
the planner prompt, leaving perception untouched, to test how much of L2 is ego-extrapolation.

\subsection{Closed-loop harness}
We built a native (Docker-free) three-process harness communicating over localhost HTTP: (i) the
NeuroNCAP orchestrator (kinematic vehicle model, collision check, NCAP scoring); (ii) the NeuRAD
renderer serving six photorealistic camera views at the ego's \emph{driven} pose each step; (iii) an
OpenDriveVLA model node that reconstructs the UniAD perception input from rendered frames
(normalization/padding, lidar-to-image projection, an 18-vector CAN-bus signal, ego-state prompt) and
exposes an \emph{in-place re-quantization} endpoint so techniques are swept without reloading. We
verified the renderer produces photorealistic frames (per-camera intensity std $25$--$49$ after
installing tiny-cuda-nn; a missing-tcnn fallback had previously rendered flat-grey blanks, and all
pre-fix closed-loop numbers were discarded).

\subsection{Metric: generation coherence}
Our primary closed-loop metric is \textbf{generation coherence}: the fraction of frames on which the
planner emits a well-formed (non-degenerate) trajectory, computed from the logged per-frame plans. A
degenerate all-zero plan corresponds to malformed/empty model output that triggers a stay-in-place
fallback. We treat collision/NCAP as a secondary, confounded proxy (Sec.~\ref{sec:closedloop}).

\subsection{On-device methodology (capture--replay)}
\label{sec:replay}
To measure accuracy on the Orin without porting UniAD's custom CUDA perception ops, we exploit that
the fused planner input (\texttt{inputs\_embeds}) is \emph{quantization-independent} (perception and
token embeddings are unquantized). We capture the fused inputs for each nuScenes sample from the A100
full pipeline, transfer them to the Orin, replay them through the decoder-only planner at each
precision, and score the emitted trajectories with the \emph{same} evaluator. This isolates device
and quantization while using the real perception features and real weights.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Q1: Open-loop L2 is blind}

Weight quantization is lossless to INT4; the INT3/INT2 degradation is generation collapse (malformed
text), not graded planning. The ego-ablation shows the metric is $\approx$ ego-extrapolation: with

cannot reflect perception/planning, nor quantization damage to them---the same phenomenon that makes
$0.5$B/$3$B/$7$B report near-identical L2 (Table~\ref{tab:planners}).

Across all 16 scenario instances, per-channel INT4 collapses generation ($\le 15\%$ well-formed on
\textbf{15/16}, mean $3.7\%$) while group-wise INT4 recovers the bulk of FP16 coherence (mean
$73.5\%$ vs $76.0\%$, $\ge 85\%$ of FP16 on \textbf{13/16}). The same per-channel INT4 is lossless
in-distribution (Table~\ref{tab:openloop}), so the failure is a quantization\,$\times$\,domain-shift
interaction, governed by the granularity mechanism of Sec.~\ref{sec:quantmethod}. Group-wise only
\emph{partially} recovers on 3 scenarios (0099, 0101, 0796); we do not claim exact equivalence.

\paragraph{Collision/NCAP is a confounded proxy.} On several scenes (e.g.\ 0796, 0278-stationary)
\emph{every} configuration including FP16 collides $10/10$: the base policy cannot clear those under
our rendering, so collisions cannot isolate quantization there even though coherence still separates
the configs. We therefore lead with coherence.

planner is a solved sub-problem.

\paragraph{On-device accuracy reproduces the A100 and the published result.} Replaying real inputs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:replay}) on the Orin over all $162$ mini-val samples yields FP16 ST-P3 L2
$0.146/0.317/0.571$\,m ($1/2/3$\,s; avg $0.345$\,m)---matching OpenDriveVLA-0.5B's reported
$0.35$\,m (Table~\ref{tab:planners})---with group-INT4 $0.151/0.327/0.583$ and per-channel-INT4
$0.156/0.362/0.652$. The Orin reproduces A100 trajectories to $\approx 1.1$\,cm mean (FP16) /
$1.5$\,cm (group-INT4). Crucially, the \emph{per-sample} W4 plans already differ from FP16 by
$14$--$24$\,cm while aggregate L2 moves $<3$\,cm: open-loop L2 averages the perturbation away
\emph{on the deployment hardware itself}.

\section{Discussion}
The study composes into one message: open-loop L2 \emph{hides} quantization damage because it is
ego-extrapolation; closed-loop \emph{reveals} it because perception and planning are on the critical
path; the revealed damage is a granularity artifact with a near-free fix; and both the blindness and
the fix reproduce on real Orin hardware. Practitioners should (i) never certify a quantized driving
policy on open-loop L2, and (ii) default to group-wise INT4 for the planner.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Scene coverage:} closed-loop spans all 14 released NeuroNCAP scenes (16 scenario instances),
not all of nuScenes. \textbf{Renderer domain gap:} rendered inputs are OOD to real nuScenes; on
several scenes even FP16 fails the collision test ($10/10$), so absolute NCAP numbers are not
in-distribution-comparable and our claims are relative. \textbf{Partial recovery:} group-wise ties/
beats FP16 on 13/16 but partially recovers on 3 (0099, 0101, 0796). \textbf{Metric proxy:} collision/
NCAP is confounded; we lead with coherence. \textbf{Fake-quant:} the static footprint is measured
on-device, but a packed-INT4 \emph{speedup} needs a custom kernel (Marlin/AWQ-style) + TensorRT and is
not yet realized---hence flat on-Orin latency. \textbf{On-Orin accuracy scope:} planner-only replay
(perception not ported to sm\_87), fp16 replay, mini-val. \textbf{AWQ/INT3/INT2:} characterized only
in an earlier single-scene, pre-renderer-fix setup and not re-reported here.

\section{Reproducibility}
The public release (\texttt{github.com/Omega1029/DrivingVLA}) includes: the open-loop eval and
quantization sweeps with the ego-ablation toggle; the three-process closed-loop harness and in-place
re-quantization service; the 4-GPU-lane 14-scene benchmark runner; the on-device suite
(\texttt{orin/bench\_planner\_orin.py}, \texttt{replay\_accuracy.py}, \texttt{subset\_l2.py},
\texttt{scene\_success.py}) and result JSONs; and the closed-loop video renderers. All numbers here
are produced by those scripts; estimates are marked.

\section{Conclusion}
For quantized autonomous-driving VLAs, open-loop L2 is blind ($19\times$ ego-ablation) and closed-loop
is necessary: there, per-channel INT4 collapses generation under deployment-domain shift while
group-wise INT4 ($\approx 0.1$ extra bits/weight) matches FP16. On a real AGX Orin the planner fits
in $2.88$\,GB, packs $3.9\times$ at group-INT4, and reproduces both the published accuracy and the
metric's blindness. Use closed-loop coherence as the metric and group-wise INT4 as the recipe.
